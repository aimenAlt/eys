## 8 Scope decomposition and geometry

Separate the customer's scope into required work, necessary implied work, optional work and unresolved work. Do not add cabinetry, premium finishes, lighting or other upgrades solely because they appear in a standing default or inspiration photo.

Use dimensional evidence in this order:

1. Direct customer measurements, labeled as customer-reported until independently verified.
2. Measurements in plans, labels, packaging or identified product specifications.
3. Clear architectural references in photos.
4. Known standard dimensions used as explicit assumptions.
5. Photo-based proportional inference with perspective considered.
6. Conservative estimating allowances.
7. A field-verification requirement where evidence remains insufficient.

Reconstruct the space across images. Do not scale a distant wall using a foreground object without accounting for perspective. Avoid fake precision. An estimate such as approximately 5–5.5 ft is appropriate when an exact measurement cannot be justified.

Calculate the job-specific quantities needed: room or wall dimensions, floor and wall area, perimeter, trim lengths, stair counts, railing runs, baluster count, cabinet components, tile or drywall surfaces and fixture counts. Document the calculation, deductions, confidence and source internally.

Use high confidence for directly measured or clearly countable information, medium confidence for strong visual inference, and low confidence for obscured or assumption-heavy estimates. A material low-confidence variable must be identified before firm pricing or procurement.

For each quantity, maintain the distinction between installed amount and purchase amount. A 31 LF rail installation may require three 12 ft lengths because of cuts. A 192 SF media-wall face does not directly determine drywall purchases: niche openings remove face area while recess backs, returns and build-out sides add surfaces.

Apply waste by material and geometry. Consider sheet layouts, stock lengths, pattern repeats, miters, fragility, stain matching and reorder consistency. Do not add waste again to a quantity that already includes a purchase allowance. Use whole package or stock quantities for purchasing and explain leftovers where meaningful.

If customer-provided numbers conflict, show the calculation and carry the uncertainty. Do not silently pick the larger number, change the geometry to reach a price or present one as measured fact. Prepare alternatives where requested and identify what must be reconciled before final approval or ordering.

## 12 Audit SimplyWise before pricing Jobber

Perform all six reviews below. Record meaningful changes in the private note.

**Scope review**
- Include necessary demolition, protection, preparation, installation, transitions, finishing, return trips, cleanup and disposal.
- Remove unrequested upgrades and distinguish required repairs from provisional concealed-condition allowances.
- Exclude demolition already completed by mitigation or another contractor.

**Quantity review**
- Ask where every significant number came from.
- Compare quantities with actual evidence and your calculations.
- Verify unit conventions such as SF, LF, EA, sheet, roll, pack, bag and gallon.
- Distinguish installed quantity, purchase allowance and lump-sum scope allowance.
- A quantity of 1 for unmeasured work means an explicit allowance, never automatically 1 SF or 1 LF.

**Labor review**
- Use man-hours, not ambiguous calendar days. Two workers for eight hours equals 16 man-hours.
- Include setup, protection, demolition, hauling, preparation, high access, detailed fitting, finishing, return mobilization and cleanup.
- Distinguish active labor from passive drying time.
- Identify whether rates represent wages, loaded EYS costs, subcontract bids or customer selling rates. Unknown basis remains unverified.
- Do not automatically increase or decrease labor; compare the actual task sequence and complexity.

**Material and product review**
- Check unit size, grade, species, thickness, package coverage and extension arithmetic.
- Detect a per-SF or per-LF price mistakenly attached to an entire sheet or stock length.
- Verify suspicious prices against appropriate current sources or use a clearly labeled realistic local allowance.
- Check compatibility of fixtures, drivers, dimmers, power supplies, mounting and finishes.
- Do not treat generic wire lists or software product suggestions as an approved electrical design.

**Duplication and markup review**
- Consolidate overlapping fasteners, tape, compound, filler, protection and masking allowances where they cover the same work.
- Avoid counting demolition, setup, finishing or cleanup in more than one task.
- Avoid counting waste both in purchases and as a separate addition.
- Remove or separately reconcile automatic markup before applying EYS pricing judgment.
- Do not blanket-mark up labor rates that already contain selling overhead without examining their basis.

**Missing work review**
- Ask what Eyad would suddenly need to buy or do if the job began tomorrow.
- Check scaffold, rental delivery, procurement, disposal, repeated travel, access, final device installation and trade coordination.
- Review remaining uncertainty, contingency and exclusions rather than burying them in invented quantities.

## 13 Cost models and selling decisions

Maintain an internal estimating model and a separate customer selling subtotal. The internal model includes materials, labor burden, subcontractors, equipment, travel, disposal and applicable direct allowances. Customer pricing also reflects overhead, coordination, warranty exposure, market value, complexity, risk and profit.

Do not describe an assumed software rate as Eyad's actual cost. Do not claim the difference between software cost and selling price is verified profit. Do not use an old job's rates or margin as a standing EYS pricing rule.

If the user has approved a price, preserve it unless the user changes it. A different SimplyWise total does not authorize changing it. If the audit exposes a material scope or cost problem, explain it before representing the approved price as adequate. If no price is approved, make a reasoned proposed price where evidence permits and label it proposed; saving a draft does not make the user approve it.

For internal reporting, let C be the audited model cost and S be the selling subtotal before the card fee:

- Added amount = S minus C.
- Markup on model cost = (S minus C) divided by C, multiplied by 100%.
- Modeled margin on selling subtotal = (S minus C) divided by S, multiplied by 100%.
- If cost or selling data are unavailable or a denominator is zero, state that the relevant percentage is not calculable.
- A target gross margin g, when authorized and supported by the cost model, implies selling price C divided by (1 minus g). Adding g percent to cost does not produce g percent margin.
- Exclude the card fee from project margin calculations.
- Label the result modeled margin when actual loaded costs, overhead, taxes, trade bids or other expenses are unverified. It is not verified net profit.

There is no confirmed universal EYS labor cost rate, automatic 20% markup or mandatory target margin in the supplied quoting memory. Do not invent one to fill the gap.

### 13.1 Independent research is required

Research the actual job independently before finalizing the proposed price. Do your own analysis of construction method, material suitability, labor burden and pricing rather than treating SimplyWise as the research or final authority. A plausible software total is not sufficient verification.

Start from your completed photo inspection and takeoff. Research enough to resolve the material cost and execution questions that could materially change this job. Depending on scope, check:

- Manufacturer installation instructions, dimensions, required clearances, substrate preparation, fastening, compatible finishes and accessory requirements.
- Current supplier prices for the right product grade, unit, stock length, package coverage and local availability; include applicable procurement taxes, delivery and minimum quantities where relevant.
- Rental/scaffold, equipment, disposal and delivery costs appropriate to site height, access and duration.
- Credible productivity information and the actual task sequence, including preparation, fitting, finish stages, protection, mobilization and cleanup.
- Relevant EYS historical actuals, if available, with adjustments for project differences, date and verified conditions.
- Credible local installed-price comparisons for genuinely similar scope in Katy/Greater West Houston, when available.
- Trade requirements and the need for a current subcontractor bid when electrical or other specialist work materially affects the estimate.

Prefer manufacturer and supplier information for product and material facts, current rental/provider information for allowances, actual EYS job records for proven productivity, and direct comparable quotes where available. Treat broad online contractor ranges as context, not evidence that an exact local project price is right. Do not mistake a labor-only rate for all-inclusive installed pricing or a national average for a Houston-area bid.

Corroborate suspicious or price-driving figures where practical. Historical prices in this guide are case evidence, not current supplier quotes.

Research is part of the estimating phase, before the final return to Jobber. Consult the local intake note rather than reopening Jobber repeatedly. External product/supplier research is expected; it does not authorize question-by-question switching between Jobber and SimplyWise. Use transparent allowances and evidence-based ranges where a preliminary estimate remains defensible. Never claim to have researched prices or confirmed a trade bid when you have not. If a price-driving unknown makes responsible pricing impossible, prepare the rest and request the required fact or bid.

### 13.2 Check for both underquoting and overquoting

Before proposing the selling subtotal, test it from both directions. A responsible price must cover the actual scoped work and reasonable business burden without inflated quantities, duplicate charges or unjustified risk padding.

**Underquoting checks**
- Have you included all necessary preparation, protection, demolition, procurement, transport, access, fitting, finishing, return visits and cleanup?
- Are purchased quantities adequate after realistic cuts, patterns, packaging and waste?
- Do labor hours reflect real complexity and active man-hours rather than optimistic calendar assumptions?
- Are subcontractors, rental delivery, disposal and applicable direct costs represented?
- Are price-driving unknowns identified and handled through defensible allowances, exclusions or agreed changes?
- Does the proposed subtotal leave an adequate amount above the supported cost model for applicable overhead, risk and profit, without pretending an unverified margin is known?

**Overquoting checks**
- Have you avoided excessive quantities, unrealistic labor allowances and charging for the same preparation or consumables twice?
- Are material grades and finishes appropriate to the requested job rather than unnecessarily premium?
- Have you excluded client-supplied purchases, unrequested upgrades and work already completed by others?
- Have you removed or reconciled automatic markup and rates that may already include overhead and selling profit?
- Is contingency proportionate to identified uncertainty rather than a hidden blanket increase?
- Does the proposed price make sense against comparable scope and local evidence, with differences explained?

Prepare a brief internal pricing reasonableness statement: audited model total; significant verified versus assumed costs; realistic low/base/high cost exposure where useful; proposed or approved subtotal; modeled margin limits; comparable local evidence; and why the selected price is defensible. Base any range on specific uncertainties such as height, substrate repair, product choice or trade bids, not arbitrary percentages.

When your model and comparable pricing disagree materially, investigate the cause before choosing a number. Check units, scope inclusions, quantities, finish quality, project size, access, geographic differences and rate basis. Do not average incompatible prices or choose the cheapest figure to appear competitive. Do not raise the price simply because SimplyWise produced a larger total.

If the user has approved a fixed price, do not change it silently. Complete the same checks, preserve the approved number in the draft, and clearly flag any material concern for the user's decision before sending or committing to the work. Do not claim that research guarantees the exact final cost or eliminates concealed-condition risk.

## 17 Detailed itemization and repair alternatives

When the user asks to re-itemize a fixed-price quote, keep the requested parent budgets and total intact. Detail is a change in presentation unless a scope or price change is explicitly authorized.

All-inclusive repair operation prices may include materials, productive installation and finishing, shared consumables, overhead and profit. Identify them as all-inclusive selling allocations. Do not misrepresent them as labor-only charges or verified cost rows.

An internal allocation method may use task weight W_i = materials + productive man-hours × loaded cost rate + allocated other direct costs. With an authorized target margin g, preliminary sell weight A_i = W_i divided by (1 minus g). Preserve an approved parent budget B by assigning S_i = B × A_i divided by the sum of A within that parent. Unit sell = S_i divided by the relevant installed quantity.

If task costs are not verified, identify weights as provisional allocations. Normalization preserves a price; it does not prove profitability or the achievement of a target margin. Actual margin still requires verified EYS costs.

Work in cents and reconcile rounding inside the appropriate parent budget. Use measured or source-reported quantities where available, with clear attribution. Use an explicit lump-sum allowance for unmeasured scope. Never invent SF, LF or a dimensioned sketch just to support unit pricing.

For repair or insurer-related presentation:

- Distinguish damaged jamb from damaged casing; one does not prove the other needs replacement.
- Distinguish cavity insulation from water-heater tank wrapping.
- Distinguish patch area, texture/blend area, full-ceiling paint area and joint length.
- Distinguish ordinary drywall backing from structural framing repair.
- Do not diagnose rot or microbial damage from discoloration alone.
- Identify remaining demolition separately from mitigation work already completed.
- Require actual evidence for framing location, member type/count, length and extent.
- Keep the card fee separate from repair costs; do not represent it as an insured repair expense.
- Clearly identify an alternative quote as an alternative, not an additional bill.
- Preserve original quotes when a separate alternative has been authorized; do not silently rewrite them.

An insurer document may supply useful quantities without resolving every scope conflict. Attribute those quantities to that document and reconcile different locations or areas before adoption. A water-heater ceiling measurement does not establish an additional wall repair area. A quantity of 2 in an original pricing row does not establish 20 LF of joints.

### 23.1 Meeka media wall

The saved records distinguish actual room images from an inspiration image and note that one actual-room image was an enlargement of another. ## 26 Jobber quote internal notes and staff only estimating breakdown

Apply this checklist together with section 16 on every quote. It makes the required estimating record explicit; local working files support the process but do not replace the saved Jobber internal note.

- Place the complete estimating breakdown in the quote's internal Notes section, visible only to EYS staff, not the customer. Verify the field's actual visibility; a private-sounding title is insufficient.
- Always use bullet points, grouped by trade or scope. Within each group, separate materials, labor and other allowances, and show the group's subtotals before the overall reconciliation.
- Include material names, purchase quantities, unit prices, extended costs and subtotals.
- Include labor tasks, man-hours, rates and totals. Distinguish internal labor costs from customer billing rates; label an unverified rate basis explicitly.
- Distinguish installed quantities from purchase quantities, including waste and cut allowances.
- Record assumptions, exclusions and items requiring onsite verification.
- Audit SimplyWise for quantity errors, unrealistic labor, incorrect unit pricing, duplicates and excessive or insufficient markup. Document meaningful corrections and their reasons.
- Show the audited estimate, selling-price calculation, 3% card-processing fee and final total. Identify proposed versus approved pricing and the limits of modeled profitability.
- Write a clear, self-contained estimating record, not conversational notes, reminders to yourself or references that require opening another file. Include the relevant quantities, cost rows, calculations and audit conclusions directly in the saved note. Links may provide supporting evidence but must not substitute for the breakdown.
- Never place internal costs, labor rates, markup calculations or estimating analysis in customer-visible line-item descriptions, Client Message or Contract / Disclaimer fields, or customer-visible attachments.
- Keep customer-facing line items concise and focused on the work included and its selling price.
- Finish all SimplyWise questions and the pricing audit first. Record the answers and prepare the complete quote before returning to Jobber.
- Fill the Jobber quote in one pass, verify the scope and displayed total, and save it as an unsent draft. Read back the saved internal note and check customer visibility. Send only after explicit user approval.
