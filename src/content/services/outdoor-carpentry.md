Elevate Your Space Handyman does the exterior woodwork side of that list — fence and gate repair, deck repair, pergolas, patio covers, and privacy screens — as measured carpentry rather than a tear-out-and-replace bid.

It is because we take the jobs in between: the single leaning section, the gate that drags, the deck that needs framing help before it needs boards, and the patio that needs a real structure built over it.

## Repair or replace — how to tell before you spend anything

This is the question behind almost every call, and the honest answer is usually cheaper than the one you will get from a company that only sells replacements.

Go out and push on a post at its base. If it moves and the wood is soft where it meets the dirt, that post is finished — but the ones on either side of it may have years left. Count how many move. A few bad posts in a hundred feet of fence is a repair, and a good one will outlast the rest of the run. If most of the line moves, or the rails are pulling away from the posts along the whole fence, you are past repair, and patching it is paying twice to delay the same job.

Decks work the same way but upside down. The boards are what you see and the framing is what matters. Soft boards over solid joists is a surface job. Soft boards because the joists underneath have been holding water is a structural job, and new boards laid over tired framing is the single most common way homeowners pay for the same deck twice. We look underneath before we quote the top.

Send photos of the full fence line or deck, plus a couple of close-ups at the base of the worst posts, and we will tell you which of these you have — before anyone drives out.

## Why outdoor wood fails here, specifically

Three things do most of the damage in this market, and understanding them is what separates a repair that holds from one you redo in two years.

**The soil line.** Posts rot at grade, not at the top. Clay holds water against the buried portion, and wet-dry cycling works the post loose in its hole. The pickets above stay presentable, which is why a fence looks fine until the day it does not. When a run has failed twice, the answer is usually to get wood out of the ground — steel posts carrying wood pickets — rather than to reset the same material in the same hole.

**Movement.** Expansive clay lifts and drops. That is why footing depth on a pergola or patio cover is not a place to save money, and why a structure set shallow racks out of square in its second summer.

**Wind.** Straight-line winds, not water, are what flattened fences across this area after Beryl in 2024 and again in the February 2026 storms. A fence that comes down in a storm usually had a post problem before the storm — the wind just found it.
