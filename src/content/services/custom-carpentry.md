If you are looking for a carpenter in Katy, you have probably found two kinds of
company. There are cabinet and millwork shops that build beautiful designed packages
and want a designed package to build. And there are large service brands that list
carpentry as item nineteen of twenty-six and send whoever is free. Elevate Your Space
sits in between: a finish carpenter who will come out for one wall, one bench, or one
set of built-ins, and treat it like the only job that week.

## When you want a carpenter, not a cabinet shop

A millwork shop is the right call when you are commissioning a kitchen, a library, or a
whole-house trim package. Their crew size, material orders and shop time are organised
around work at that scale, which is exactly why a request to build one bench beside the
back door tends to go unanswered.

Most homeowners are not commissioning a package. They have a specific wall that has
bothered them for two years — the dead space beside the fireplace, the alcove under the
stairs, the entry where backpacks land on the floor. That is a two or three day job for
one carpenter with the right tools, and it is the work we are set up for.

## Matching what is already there

Most of the craft in finish carpentry is in the last quarter inch. A new piece has to
meet an existing baseboard, an existing casing and an existing paint sheen without
announcing where one ends and the other begins.

Production homes in Katy, Fulshear and Richmond were trimmed with stock profiles, so
matching is usually a question of identifying the profile, not milling a new one. Walls
in those homes are also rarely square — which is why we build in the room and scribe the
piece to the wall it meets, instead of delivering something that fits perfectly in a
shop and leaves a tapered gap in your house.

Gulf Coast humidity swings matter too. Wood moves with the season here, so joints are
cut and fastened with that movement in mind rather than glued rigid and left to open in
January.

## Paint-grade, stain-grade, and what it changes

Paint-grade work is usually poplar or MDF. It is smooth, stable, cheaper, and the paint
hides the grain, so it is the right choice for most built-ins, paneling and trim.

Stain-grade is oak, maple or walnut, and every board has to be chosen and arranged so
the grain reads well across the piece, because the finish conceals nothing. It costs
more in material and considerably more in labour. It is worth it when the wood itself is
the feature — a floating mantel, an open shelf at eye level, a slat wall.

We will tell you which one your project actually needs, including when the honest answer
is the cheaper one.

## Getting an estimate

Send photos of the wall and a rough width and height. That is enough for a written
estimate with the build plan in it — material, finish, how many days, and how the piece
meets your existing trim.

If the carpentry is one item on a longer list of small jobs, a
