## The one thing worth checking before you buy a fan

Take a photo of the ceiling box before you order anything. Two things decide most of
what follows.

The first is whether the box is rated for a fan. A fan-rated box is marked and is
fastened to a joist or to a brace spanning between joists. A standard light box is held
by nails or a thin bracket and is not built to carry something that moves. Hanging a fan
on a light box is the most common reason a fan eventually comes down, and it is why a
fan that "was fine for years" is not evidence that it was installed correctly.

The second is whether the room has a separate switch for the light. Plenty of rooms have
one switch running to the box. If you want the light and the fan controlled
independently, that either means a fan with a remote or additional wiring, and it is
much cheaper to know before you buy than afterwards.

## Getting the height right

The usual target is blade height eight to nine feet off the floor. On a standard
eight-foot ceiling that means a flush or hugger mount. Around nine feet takes roughly a
six-inch downrod, ten feet about twelve inches, and a twelve-foot ceiling around
twenty-four.

Hugger mounts on higher ceilings are the most common mistake we are called to correct.
The fan is safe and it looks fine, but the air never reaches anyone, so the room stays
warm and the fan gets blamed.

## Sizing the fan to the room

Up to 75 square feet suits a 29 to 36 inch blade span. From 76 to 144 square feet, 36 to
44 inches. From 145 to 225, 44 to 54 inches. Larger rooms want 50 inches and up, and a
long living room often does better with two smaller fans than one oversized one.

## Wobble, hum, and fans that have stopped

Wobble is usually blades that are not weighted evenly, a bent blade holder, a loose
canopy, or a box that was never level. It is rarely dangerous and almost always fixable
in a short visit. A hum is often a dimmer being used on a fan motor, which is the wrong
control for the job. Either way it is worth a call before you go and buy a replacement
you may not need.

## Covered patios and porches

A covered patio needs a damp-rated fan at minimum, and anywhere blowing rain reaches
needs wet-rated. Indoor fans put outside in this climate corrode at the blade holders
and go out of balance within a season or two. It is one of the few places where buying
the cheaper fan reliably costs more.
