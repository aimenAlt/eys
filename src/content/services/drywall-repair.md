From a doorknob through the hallway wall to the nail pops that reappear every spring,
Elevate Your Space repairs drywall across Katy, Cypress, Cinco Ranch, Fulshear,
Richmond and West Houston. The standard we work to is simple: when the room is finished,
you should not be able to find the repair from across it in daylight.

## Do you actually need a drywall company?

If a ceiling has come down, a room is being re-hung, or you have water damage across
several sheets, call a drywall specialist. That is what they are set up for and they are
good at it.

Most calls are not that. Most calls are six nail pops, a doorknob hole, two anchor holes
where a shelf used to be, and a crack over the pantry door. A drywall company can do
that work, but a half-day of patching is an awkward booking for a crew that prices by
the room — so it sits, and you wait. That list is a normal day for us, and it usually
comes with a TV to re-hang or a door that sticks, which we handle in the same visit.

## Why a patch shows, and how it is avoided

Three things give a repair away, and all three are avoidable.

**Texture.** Orange peel and knockdown are the textures on most walls here. Matching
knockdown is about the timing of the knockdown pass rather than the compound — go too
early and it smears, too late and it stays proud.

**Feathering.** Compound has to be carried well past the patch and thinned to nothing.
Stop short and the wall gets a visible halo that only appears once the paint is on and
the light is right.

**Sheen.** Touch-up paint from the same can will still read differently if the wall has
aged, and eggshell shows the join far more than flat. Sometimes the honest answer is
that the wall needs painting corner to corner, and we would rather say so up front than
hand you a patch you notice every morning.

## Nail pops, and why they come back

This is the most common repeat call we get. Filling a nail pop does nothing about the
fastener that pushed it out.

Clay soils across Katy and Fulshear swell and shrink with the seasons. The framing moves
with the ground, and a fastener that is only holding by friction works its way loose
again. The repair that holds is a new fastener driven into the stud beside the pop, then
both dimpled and patched together. It takes slightly longer once and does not come back
next spring.

## Cracks worth a second opinion

Hairline cracks running diagonally from the corners of doors and windows are ordinary
seasonal movement and are a patching job.

A few things are worth having someone else look at before anyone puts compound on them:
stair-step cracks in the exterior brick, doors or windows that have stopped latching,
cracks wider than about a quarter of an inch, or a crack you can see has grown in the
last few months. We will tell you when we think that is the case. Patching over
something structural hides it for one season and costs you twice.

## Water-stained ceilings

The stain is the last part of the problem, not the first. Find and fix the source, let
the area dry fully, then seal with a stain-blocking primer before any paint goes on —
ordinary paint lets a water stain bleed straight back through within weeks. Once it is
sealed, the ceiling is repaired and finished like any other.
